If you've ever tried tracking your pantry in a spreadsheet, you know how it ends: enthusiasm for two weeks, then abandonment. A real **pantry inventory app** has to be faster than not tracking at all.

This is what to look for — and how PantryOS approaches each requirement.

## The 5 things that actually matter

### 1. Sub-3-second logging

If adding an item takes longer than just putting it on the shelf, you'll stop using the app within a month. Look for:

- Barcode scanning (camera or hardware)
- Auto-fill of name, brand, image from a product database
- Quantity +/- with one tap

### 2. Multi-location support

Real homes have multiple "pantries": cupboard, fridge, freezer, garage shelves. The app must treat them as separate spaces with their own inventory.

### 3. Shared household state

Inventory only works if **everyone in the house** sees the same data, in real time. Solo apps fail because the partner who shops at lunch doesn't open them.

### 4. Expiration awareness

Tracking what you have without tracking *when* it expires is half a solution. You want:

- Per-item expiration dates
- Notifications N days before
- A "use soon" view

### 5. Export and data ownership

Your inventory is yours. The app should let you export to CSV/Excel/JSON anytime — no lock-in.

## Common pitfalls

**Pitfall 1: Apps built around shopping lists.** A shopping list is downstream of inventory. If the app starts there, inventory will always feel like an afterthought.

**Pitfall 2: No offline.** Pantries are sometimes in basements with bad Wi-Fi. The scanner should work offline and sync later.

**Pitfall 3: Per-user accounts only.** Forces every family member to register and creates two parallel inventories that drift apart.
