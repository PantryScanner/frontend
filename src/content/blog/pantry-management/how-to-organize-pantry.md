A disorganized pantry costs you **time, money, and wasted food**. The good news: two hours of work, then five minutes a week to maintain it.

Here's the method we use internally at PantryOS — works with or without the app.

## 1. Empty everything

Yes, everything. Put it on the kitchen table. It's the only way to see what you actually own — and what you forgot you had three years ago.

> Roughly 30% of what comes out is expired. That's not a failure, it's your baseline.

## 2. Group by use, not by shape

The classic mistake: arranging "jars with jars" because they look nice together. Wrong. Group by **when you use them**:

- Breakfast (coffee, cereal, jam)
- Quick meals (pasta, sauces, canned goods)
- Baking (flour, sugar, leaveners)
- Spices and condiments
- Snacks
- Drinks

## 3. One zone = one category

Assign each category a fixed shelf. When groceries come in, **you shouldn't have to think** about where things go.

### The "front of house" principle

The eye-level shelf is your most valuable real estate. Put daily items there. The whole-wheat pasta you only cook on Sundays can live higher up.

## 4. FIFO: First In, First Out

When new groceries arrive, **push the old stock forward** and put the new behind it. This single habit eliminates 80% of expiration waste.

| Look here first | Put here |
|---|---|
| Front | Older items (use first) |
| Back | New items |

## 5. Clear containers, big labels

Decanting flour, rice, and legumes into clear containers means:

- You see how much is left at a glance
- No moths, no moisture
- Recognize categories without reading

Label every container with **name + open date**.

## 6. Build a "use-soon" bin

A small basket for items **close to expiring**. Cook those first. Period.

## 7. Track it (even mentally)

This is where digital tools earn their keep. If you know what you have, you stop:

- Buying a third can of tomatoes
- Running out of coffee on Sunday morning
- Throwing away expired yogurt

> **With PantryOS** the mobile scanner lets you log items in or out in 2 seconds. The pantry tracks itself.

## TL;DR

1. Empty everything
2. Group by use
3. One zone = one category
4. FIFO always
5. Clear containers
6. Use-soon bin
7. Track digitally

Maintenance is easy: 5 minutes a week to check the use-soon bin and re-FIFO after groceries.
