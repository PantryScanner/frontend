The average household throws away roughly **30% of the food it buys**. That's around €1,200 a year for a family of four, plus the environmental cost.

The interesting part: most food waste isn't a values problem. It's a **systems problem**. Fix the system and the waste drops by itself.

## Why food gets wasted (the real reasons)

1. **Forgetting what you have** → duplicate purchases
2. **Buying too much** → things spoil before you use them
3. **Storing badly** → faster spoilage
4. **Not seeing expirations** → things expire unnoticed
5. **Over-prepping** → leftovers nobody eats

Each one has a concrete fix.

## The 12 changes

### Cut duplicates

1. **Inventory before shopping.** 60 seconds of "do we still have rice?" prevents the second bag of rice.
2. **Photo the fridge before leaving.** If you don't track digitally, at least carry a photo.
3. **Use a shared shopping list app.** No more two people buying the same eggs.

### Buy less, more often

4. **Plan 5 dinners, not 7.** Leave 2 slots for leftovers or improvisation.
5. **Skip "bulk deals" you can't finish.** 3-for-2 yogurts that expire in 4 days are a 33% saving and a 33% waste.

### Store better

6. **Fridge zones matter.** Top shelf for ready-to-eat, bottom for raw meat, drawers for produce. Wrong zone = 2x faster spoilage.
7. **Freeze bread the day you buy it.** Slice it first.
8. **Herbs in water like flowers.** Triples their life.

### Track expirations

9. **First-In-First-Out (FIFO).** New stock goes behind old stock. Every time.
10. **Use-soon basket.** A dedicated zone for items within 3 days of expiring. Cook from it first.
11. **Set reminders 3 days before.** Manually, or via a [pantry app with expiration notifications](/blog/pantry-management/pantry-inventory-app).

### Eat what you have

12. **"Empty fridge" night once a week.** Force yourself to cook only from what's already there. Surprisingly fun.

## What good looks like

After 4 weeks of these habits, most households report:

- **−50% to −70%** of weekly food thrown away
- **€80–€150/month** saved on groceries
- Less mental load (no more "what's for dinner" panic)

## Where tracking helps

The single highest-leverage change is **knowing what you have**. Once you can see your inventory at a glance:

- You stop buying duplicates
- You see what's expiring before it does
- You build meals around what's already there
